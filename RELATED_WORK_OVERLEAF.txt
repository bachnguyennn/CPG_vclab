\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Mask-based continual learning without forgetting}
Regularization-based continual learners such as elastic weight consolidation
(EWC)~\cite{kirkpatrick2017ewc} penalize movement of important weights but only
\emph{discourage} forgetting; a
second family removes forgetting \emph{by construction} through parameter
isolation. PackNet~\cite{mallya2018packnet} iteratively prunes a network and
assigns the surviving weights to a task, freezing them permanently so later
tasks cannot overwrite them. Piggyback~\cite{mallya2018piggyback} learns a
binary mask over a fixed backbone per task, reusing frozen weights without
modifying them. Compacting, Picking and Growing (CPG)~\cite{hung2019cpg} unifies
these ideas: each task trains only free weights, \emph{picks} (read-only reuses)
old weights through a learned binary ``piggymask,'' \emph{compacts} its own
weights by gradual pruning, and \emph{grows} the network when the free pool is
exhausted. Because forgetting is prevented by exact weight freezing, the
guarantee is falsifiable at the bit level. This thesis takes CPG as its
mechanism and ports it, unchanged, onto a modern hybrid vision transformer.

\subsection{Efficient hybrid vision transformer backbones}
EfficientViT~\cite{liu2023efficientvit} introduces a memory-efficient sandwich
layout and \emph{cascaded group attention} (CGA), in which the token feature is
split into per-head channel chunks and each head's output is cascaded into the
next, cutting attention redundancy and FLOPs. CascadedViT
(CViT)~\cite{sivakumar2025cascadedvit} extends this lineage with a cascaded
chunk feed-forward network (CCFFN), reducing parameters by ${\sim}20\%$ and
FLOPs by ${\sim}15\%$ over its EfficientViT backbone while targeting
battery-constrained deployment. The contiguous-chunk routing in CGA and CCFFN is
the central architectural fact of this work: it is what breaks naive
width-based network growing and dictates the unit-granular growth axis we adopt.

\subsection{Parameter-efficient per-task adaptation}
As an alternative to weight-ownership masks, low-rank adaptation
(LoRA)~\cite{hu2022lora} freezes the backbone and learns a low-rank update
$\Delta W = BA$ per task. LoRA gives a naturally forgetting-free per-task
adapter and serves as our primary baseline for the accuracy-versus-storage
trade-off against CPG masks on the identical frozen backbone.

\subsection{Compressing the per-task footprint}
Both masks and adapters incur a per-task storage floor. BitDelta%
~\cite{liu2024bitdelta} shows that the delta between a fine-tuned model and its
base survives quantization to a single sign bit per weight plus one scale per
tensor, on the premise that fine-tuning adds little information over the base.
The per-task stores here have exactly that base-plus-small-delta structure, so
we port the BitDelta treatment to compress the trained floor on both the CPG and
LoRA arms.

\subsection{Foundational components and benchmark}
The reference reproduction uses the VGG-16 backbone~\cite{simonyan2015vgg} on
which CPG was originally validated. The CViT backbone relies on batch
normalization~\cite{ioffe2015batchnorm} and squeeze-and-excitation
modules~\cite{hu2018senet}; the piggymask and BitDelta stores both use the
straight-through estimator~\cite{bengio2013ste} to backpropagate through
binarization/quantization. All continual-learning experiments follow the CPG
Experiment-1 protocol on CIFAR-100~\cite{krizhevsky2009cifar} (the 100 fine
classes grouped into 20 semantic-superclass tasks), with backbones optionally
pretrained on ImageNet~\cite{deng2009imagenet}.
